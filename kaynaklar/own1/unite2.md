We use already to explain that something happened
before we expected or to emphasise it has happened.
We normally put already between have and the past
participle.
She has already bought a ticket. | have already seen
the show.
We use still in negative sentences to express that
something we expected has not happened but we
imagine it will happen in the future. We put still
directly after the subject.
My uncle still hasn’t seen the new play.
We use yet in negative sentences to emphasise that
something we expected has not happened. We put yet
after the complete verb phrase.
John hasn’t arrived yet.

We use yet in questions to ask if something has
happened before now. It comes at the end of the
question.
Have you bought the bus tickets yet?
